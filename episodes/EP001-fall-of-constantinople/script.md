## Chapter 1 — The case file

### Scene 1.1 — The walls, the case, the night
[SET: biển + bán đảo từ trên cao, tường ba lớp; camera hạ xuống]
NARRATOR: For more than a thousand years, nothing could break the walls of Constantinople. Arab armies tried, twice. Fleets tried. A Crusader army got in, in twelve oh four, though that one hardly counts, since it was supposedly on the city's side.
NARRATOR: Then, in the spring of fourteen fifty-three, a twenty-one-year-old sultan parked his army outside them. In under eight weeks, the Roman Empire was over.
<!-- B#1–4, WHE 674–678/717–718, 1204 -->

[ACTION: ba mô hình đứng trên bàn hồ sơ: khẩu pháo, cánh cửa nhỏ, người đàn ông băng bó]
NARRATOR: Ask historians what broke those walls and you won't get an answer. You'll get an argument. Three suspects. A cannon. A small door that somebody forgot to lock. And one wounded man, carried off the wall at exactly the wrong moment.
NARRATOR: The witnesses are not helpful. One is a Venetian who disliked the Genoese. One is a Genoese archbishop who disliked the Venetians. And one is a Greek chronicler with firm opinions about everybody. By the end, I'll tell you which suspect I would arrest. Or why the question might be slightly wrong.

## Chapter 2 — The last Roman

### Scene 2.1 — A very small empire (RECON)
[SET: sảnh cung, bản đồ trải trên bàn; Constantine, Sphrantzes]
NARRATOR: His name was Constantine Palaiologos. Constantine the Eleventh, if you're counting, and the Byzantines most certainly were. Born in fourteen oh four, the fourth son of an emperor, which is not a position that comes with high expectations. His brother John died without an heir in October fourteen forty-eight. In January fourteen forty-nine, Constantine was proclaimed emperor in the Peloponnese. Never crowned in Hagia Sophia, as custom required. Which tells you how the empire was doing.
<!-- B#7 -->
NARRATOR: Nobody recorded the exact words, so imagine the scene something like this.
SPHRANTZES: Majesty, the inventory of your empire.
CONSTANTINE: Show me.
SPHRANTZES: Here. Constantinople. A few islands. And the southern Peloponnese.
CONSTANTINE: And the rest?
SPHRANTZES: That is the rest.
CONSTANTINE: It's a very small map.
SPHRANTZES: I could draw it larger, Majesty. It would not improve the content.
<!-- B#3 -->

NARRATOR: The Romans still called themselves Romans, by the way. A thousand years after Rome had stopped being theirs to call. And Constantine had a second problem: no children. Every attempt to find him a wife, in Aragon, in Trebizond, in Georgia, fell through.
<!-- B#8 -->
SPHRANTZES: Aragon has declined.
CONSTANTINE: I see.
SPHRANTZES: Georgia is, ah, considering.
CONSTANTINE: For how long?
SPHRANTZES: They did not say. Trebizond is also considering.
CONSTANTINE: Everyone is considering me. It's flattering. It's also not a marriage.
NARRATOR: An emperor with no heir, no wife, and not much of an empire. But he did have the walls.

### Scene 2.2 — How to read a wall (FICT, Q&A)
[SET: tường Theodosian ban ngày, mặt cắt 3D: hào → tường ngoài → tường trong; Dimitri mới nhận ca gác, Stavros nhai bánh mì]
NARRATOR: So let's talk about those walls, because everything hangs on them. We'll let Dimitri ask. It's his first week.
DIMITRI: How high is it?
STAVROS: Which part?
DIMITRI: The… wall.
STAVROS: There are three. First, a ditch. About sixty feet wide.
DIMITRI: Sixty feet of nothing?
STAVROS: Sixty feet of nobody-wants-to-cross-this. Then the outer wall. Twenty-five feet. Then the inner wall, forty feet and more, with towers.
DIMITRI: And the enemy has to get through all of it.
STAVROS: Under fire.
DIMITRI: How long has it stood?
STAVROS: Since the fifth century.
DIMITRI: And nobody's broken it?
STAVROS: Nobody. Don't say it too loudly.

DIMITRI: Where's the weak part?
STAVROS: Why would I tell you that?
DIMITRI: Because I'm standing on it?
STAVROS: Down there. Where the little river cuts through. The Lycus. The soft part. The Greeks call it the Mesoteichion.
DIMITRI: What does that mean?
STAVROS: "Middle wall."
DIMITRI: That doesn't sound frightening.
STAVROS: It will.
NARRATOR: Remember that name. The soft part. We'll be back.

## Chapter 3 — The boy sultan

### Scene 3.1 — A fortress for the strait (RECON ⚠)
[SET: tòa hội đồng ở Edirne, tấm bản đồ eo biển; Mehmed đứng, Halil ngồi]
NARRATOR: Meanwhile, in Edirne, the Ottoman capital, a young man was making plans. Mehmed the Second was born in fourteen thirty-two. He was already a sultan once, briefly, as a boy. In fourteen fifty-one he took the throne again, and this time he meant it.
<!-- B#4 -->
NARRATOR: What was said in that council, we can only reconstruct. It probably went something like this.
MEHMED: There is a narrow place on the Bosphorus. Six hundred and sixty meters. I want a fortress on it.
HALIL: My lord, with respect, that land belongs to the Greeks.
MEHMED: Which Greeks?
HALIL: The ones with the city. The one we are… standing next to.
MEHMED: Then they will not object. They haven't the soldiers.
HALIL: They will say it breaks a treaty.
MEHMED: Then they will say it. Slowly. In writing.
HALIL: My lord, the last time you were sultan, you were twelve.
MEHMED: And now I am nineteen.
HALIL: Twenty.
MEHMED: Then I have even more experience than I thought.
<!-- RECON; Mehmed b. 30/3/1432, first reign 1444, second 1451 (WP); lập trường Halil ⚠ verify -->

SPHRANTZES: On the strait. Our strait.
CONSTANTINE: We have a strait?
SPHRANTZES: We had one, Majesty. It is now under construction.

## Chapter 4 — The gunfounder

### Scene 4.1 — A very good cannon, no budget (RECON)
[SET: sảnh nhỏ; Urban bên một mô hình nòng pháo; Constantine, Sphrantzes]
NARRATOR: Now the most expensive job interview in medieval history. A Hungarian engineer called Urban, who cast bronze cannon, arrived in Constantinople looking for work.
NARRATOR: The chroniclers give us the outcome, not the dialogue. But the sequence of events was very close to this.
URBAN: Majesty. I make the biggest guns in the world. One shot, and the wall—
CONSTANTINE: I have walls. I need a way to keep them.
URBAN: Then you will want a gun, to frighten the enemy.
CONSTANTINE: Perhaps. What is your fee?
URBAN: Regular wages. Regular supplies. Regular bronze.
SPHRANTZES: Bronze costs money.
CONSTANTINE: And so, regrettably, do you.
URBAN: Surely, for an emperor—
CONSTANTINE: I can offer gratitude.
SPHRANTZES: Gratitude is not on the payroll.
<!-- RECON; Runciman/Pears 231–232: emperor could not pay or supply -->

[SET: lều Mehmed, mô hình pháo, ánh đèn]
NARRATOR: Urban went down the road to the sultan.
MEHMED: How much do you want?
URBAN: Four times what the emperor offered, and—
MEHMED: Done.
URBAN: —with respect, my lord, I have not told you what it can do.
MEHMED: Tell me.
URBAN: It can break the walls of Babylon.
MEHMED: I'm not interested in Babylon.
URBAN: Of course. The walls of Constantinople, then.
MEHMED: Better.

### Scene 5.2 — Seven a day (FICT)
[SET: Mesoteichion, đêm 6/4; cú nổ đầu tiên; Dimitri, Stavros]
NARRATOR: The siege began on the sixth of April. Mehmed's great cannon opened the wall and, as promised, it did so about seven times a day. Every night, the defenders went out and filled the hole.
DIMITRI: Stavros, what are they putting in the gap?
STAVROS: Stones. Timber. Earth. Barrels.
DIMITRI: And those?
STAVROS: Animal hides.
DIMITRI: Why hides?
STAVROS: Soft things swallow cannonballs.
DIMITRI: So we're defending the empire with a sofa.
STAVROS: A very good sofa.
DIMITRI: Do we get to sit on it?
STAVROS: Eat your bread.
<!-- Pears 307–310; B#16: ~7 shots/day -->
NARRATOR: It sounds ridiculous. It worked. Stone shatters. A pile of earth, barrels and leather mostly just gives up the ball and goes on being a pile. For six weeks, a thousand years of masonry was being held together by furniture.

[SET: lều Mehmed, đèn dầu; Mehmed, chỉ huy]

MEHMED: And the wall?
COMMANDER: Damaged, my lord. And in the morning, mended.
MEHMED: Mended with what?

MEHMED: Then bring more cannon.
COMMANDER: We are bringing more cannon.
MEHMED: Then bring them faster.

Before them, the harbor. And between them, the whole day depended on the wind. It dropped. The big ships stalled. The Ottoman galleys swarmed. And then the wind came back, at exactly the right time, and the four ships broke through.

## Chapter 6 — Omens

[SET: đêm sương, đuốc rước, ánh sáng lạ trên mái Hagia Sophia]

### Scene 7.4 — Exhibit A: the cannon
[SET: bàn hồ sơ; mô hình khẩu pháo; NARRATOR là thanh tra]
NARRATOR: Exhibit A. The cannon. Did it break the wall? Yes. For almost seven weeks, it opened breach after breach, and a stone from Urban's gun could flatten a section of battlements. Did it break the city? No. The defenders fixed the walls with barrels and earth every night. The cannon made the problem. It could not, on its own, finish the job.
<!-- B#16, D8 -->

### Scene 7.5 — Exhibit B: the wounded man (RECON)
[SET: Mesoteichion, trước bình minh; ánh sáng xám; Giustiniani trúng đạn; Constantine chạy đến]
NARRATOR: Exhibit B. The wounded man. Before dawn on the twenty-ninth, a shot struck Giustiniani, nearly through his breastplate. He was bleeding badly. He wanted to go.
<!-- Runciman 163 -->
NARRATOR: No one wrote down the words. But several sources agree that the emperor begged him to stay.
GIUSTINIANI: I'm hit. I need a surgeon.
CONSTANTINE: Stay. Without you, the wall goes with you.
GIUSTINIANI: Majesty, I can't stand.
CONSTANTINE: Then sit. But sit here.
GIUSTINIANI: I'm not asking.
CONSTANTINE: The Genoese will see you go.
GIUSTINIANI: They will see a man who's been shot.
<!-- RECON; Runciman: emperor begged him; Barbaro: faked; Philippides & Hanak reject -->
NARRATOR: His bodyguards carried him through a gate and down to a Genoese ship. He died on Chios a day or two later. The Venetian Barbaro, who disliked the Genoese, said it was fear. Most modern historians don't. Either way, the Genoese on the wall saw the man they trusted most leave it.

### Scene 7.6 — Exhibit C: the little door (RECON/FICT)
[SET: Kerkoporta, góc tường Blachernae; Dimitri, Stavros, và lính Genoa đang tràn qua]
NARRATOR: Exhibit C. The door. A small gate near the corner of the walls, the Kerkoporta. Ducas, the Greek chronicler, says it was left open by mistake. Runciman says it was a sally port that defenders used, and that when Giustiniani was carried off, the Genoese streamed through it in confusion. Critobulus doesn't mention it at all. One source. Maybe none.
<!-- B#23; Pears 342–345; Runciman -->
DIMITRI: Who's closing the door?
STAVROS: I thought you were.
DIMITRI: I thought you were.
STAVROS: Then it's open.
[ACTION: cờ Ottoman xuất hiện trên tháp phía trên cửa]
VOICE: The city is taken!
DIMITRI: Who said that?
STAVROS: Someone who looked up.
NARRATOR: A flag on a tower and a cry in the dark. That was the moment, in some accounts. In others, it was only the moment people believed it.

NARRATOR: Meanwhile, at the stockade, the third wave arrived. The Janissaries, the sultan's own troops, came forward in order, with discipline. Their leader, a man called Hassan, was first onto the stockade, and was killed there. The rest came over him.

STAVROS: And then you hit the next one.
DIMITRI: And after that?
STAVROS: Then it's time for the bread.

### Scene 7.7 — The emperor (DOC)
[SET: cổng Romanus; Constantine xuống ngựa cùng ba người]
NARRATOR: Constantine heard the shout. According to Runciman, he dismounted with three companions, Theophilus Palaiologos, Don Francisco of Toledo, and John Dalmata, and stood at the gate where Giustiniani had been carried off.

## Chapter 8 — What came after

### Scene 8.1 — The witnesses (DOC)
[SET: bục nhân chứng; NARRATOR hỏi cung; các nhân chứng nối nhau]
NARRATOR: Nobody knows how Constantine died. We have eight accounts. I'll call a few.
<!-- Pears 352–357: "no source entirely reliable; the grave is unknown" -->
WITNESS: He died fighting. — Critobulus.
WITNESS: A Janissary struck him down. — Leonard of Chios.
WITNESS: His head was recognized by Notaras. — Ducas.
WITNESS: He killed three Turks, then lost his head. — Pusculus.
WITNESS: I heard he was trampled in a gate. Not sure. — Barbaro.
NARRATOR: Eight accounts. Eight deaths. Nobody knows where he lies.

### Scene 8.2 — The city is his (DOC/RECON)
[SET: Hagia Sophia, chiều; Mehmed vào cổng bằng ngựa; binh lính cạy đá cẩm thạch]
NARRATOR: Mehmed waited until the first wave of looting had calmed, and rode into the city in the afternoon. In Hagia Sophia, he found a soldier prying up the marble floor.
<!-- Runciman: entered later; Pears 372–374 -->
MEHMED: The plunder is yours. The buildings are mine.
<!-- RECON; Pears: "kiến trúc dành cho ta" -->
NARRATOR: Walking through the empty palace, he recited a line of Persian poetry about the spider weaving the curtain in the palace of the Caesars.
<!-- Pears 374 -->
NARRATOR: The killing in the morning was bad. Then something odd happened. The soldiers worked out that a prisoner was worth more than a corpse. That kind of economic insight saves some lives and ruins the rest. Mehmed had promised his men three days of plunder, as custom held, and by one account he stopped it after the first.
<!-- Pears 358–364, 368–369; B#26 D -->
NARRATOR: Critobulus counts about four thousand dead, and more than fifty thousand taken captive.
<!-- D9; Critobulus & WHE -->
NARRATOR: And Notaras, the Grand Duke with the district? Ducas says he was executed after refusing to hand over his son. Critobulus says he faced death calmly with his sons. Both stories are disputed, and one modern historian suspects the son detail is a storytelling habit.
<!-- B#31; Pears 374–377: D/L -->
NARRATOR: Then Mehmed appointed Gennadius, the monk who'd warned them, as patriarch of the Greek Church.
MEHMED: Be patriarch, and be at peace.
<!-- Pears 381–384 (E) -->
NARRATOR: And Mehmed became Qayser-i Rûm. Caesar of Rome.
<!-- B#32 -->

### Scene 8.3 — The verdict
[SET: bàn hồ sơ; ba mô hình xếp thành chuỗi: pháo → vết thương → cánh cửa]
NARRATOR: So, which suspect would I arrest? None of them. The cannon broke the wall. The wound broke the line. The door, or the idea of a door, let them in. They're not three suspects. They're one chain. Runciman himself ended his book saying that, in the long run, little would have changed.
<!-- Runciman conclusion -->
NARRATOR: Every tower had two men like Dimitri and Stavros. The chroniclers kept the numbers of the dead and the captured. They didn't keep the names. But somewhere on that wall, somebody left half a loaf of bread. The walls are still standing. Go and look.
